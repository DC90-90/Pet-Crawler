Findings and their causes

Critical findings can directly invalidate pricing decisions, create fabricated metrics or expose production changes. “Contained locally” describes the included patch, not a deployed fix.

CriticalLive + source + reproductionRelease blocker

F01 · A variant barcode can inherit another variant’s price

The crawler selects the first numeric variant identifier and its price but retains the parent SKU and parent quantity. It then stores all variant identifiers in arrays. The matcher and Market Share can match any identifier in those arrays to the same single snapshot price. Reversing two variants in a fixture changed the price from 20 to 200 under the same ROOT SKU. A 99% identity score does not validate the price’s variant.

Repair: Persist one offer per store, listing and variant, with that variant’s own price, quantity, attributes and source evidence. Parent arrays may discover candidates but must never supply a comparable price. Quarantine unresolved variants; do not repair this with product-specific price exceptions.

F02 · Production can generate fabricated historical observations

On an empty stores collection, SEED_DEMO_DATA defaults to true. The exact current seed function generated 31,450 backdated snapshots for 202 products across seven stores in an isolated in-memory test. 27,133 received confidence ≥85. The original seed does not label those observations synthetic. This proves a fabrication mechanism, not that every live historical point came from it.

Repair: The patch defaults seeding off, requires explicit development/test opt-in and labels generated snapshots synthetic. Inspect a backed-up production database for seed provenance before quarantining any existing records. Never reconstruct missing historical prices by randomization or interpolation presented as observations.

Pinned source: backend/server.py:612

CriticalSource + reproductionPartially contained locally

F03 · Missing observations manufacture sales

Quantity [20, null] becomes 20 estimated sales. Counter [100, null, 100] becomes 50 estimated sales because null is converted to zero and the apparent increase is clipped. Quantity [40, 0] produces 40 despite the advertised 10-unit interval cap. Filtering missing/placeholder readings can also bridge intervals that were never continuously observed.

Repair: The patch preserves gaps in the shared estimator and daily rollup, rejects implausible jumps instead of inventing a clipped count, and retains genuine observed zero. Upstream extractors still collapse some missing fields into zero, and other legacy velocity paths remain: migrate them to one typed observation contract before release.

Pinned source: backend/core/utils.py:187; backend/server.py:4120

CriticalLive + sourceContained locally

F04 · Store capability is incorrectly treated as product-level zero sales

If any product proves a store publishes stock/counters and the store has two days of data, _resolve_units returns zero for another product with no sales row. That product may never have been observed twice. The live UI calls this “Measured zero” and says the product genuinely did not move. Unchanged inventory also cannot rule out sales followed by restocking.

Repair: The patch returns Unavailable when product-level evidence is missing. Exact zero requires a complete order-feed window or an explicitly defined observation-only metric; it cannot be inferred from another product’s signal.

Pinned source: backend/market_share.py:237

CriticalSource + reproductionRelease blocker

F05 · Merchant-local SKUs and manual decisions leak across stores

products is looked up by SKU alone. Two stores’ LOCAL-100 fixtures—cat food at 25 and a dog cage at 500—share one catalogue product ID. A contradictory store barcode can be overridden by the shared catalogue barcode. A manual confirmation keyed by competitor SKU applies to a different store. Exact proprietary SKU equality matched different brands with conflicting barcodes at confidence 95. SKU-level deduplication drops a second retailer, and an early barcode return skips other stores’ SKU matches.

Repair: Store-scoped offer identity and explicit catalogue mappings are required. Key approvals/blacklists by own product plus competitor store, listing and variant. Validate GTIN check digits and reject conflicting pack/size evidence. Merchant SKU equality should only propose a match unless corroborated.

F06 · Prices do not share a consistent eligibility rule across screens

The sampled 20 kg litter row used Lana’s 13-day-old SAR 29.95 price. Detail also showed Zarafa’s April 25 observation and two out-of-stock offers. The detail rank included six sellers. “All data fresh” describes store runs, not the age or validity of each contributing offer. HighLive + source + reproductionPartially contained locally

F07 · Sales proxies and incomplete denominators are presented too strongly

Stock depletion is not a sales floor: transfers, damage, reservations and corrections can reduce stock without sales. Revenue uses shelf prices, not invoice prices. The legacy My Products calculation can combine 20 actual own units with a 10-unit snapshot denominator and display 200%, marked non-estimated. Ranking labels any numeric revenue_30d “exact.” In live Market Share, 2,832 own products and 1,358 with a tracked competitor imply 1,474 without one, yet 1,765 were labelled “Only I sell it”: the code conflates missing competitor sales with absent competitors.

Repair: The patch withholds the unreconciled legacy share, labels revenue proxies as estimates, separates catalogue presence from sales availability, and aligns headline numerator/denominator cohorts. Remaining estimates need per-product coverage and uncertainty. Exact own invoices do not make an estimated competitor denominator exact.

Pinned source: backend/server.py:2792; backend/server.py:7325; backend/market_share.py:438

HighLive + sourcePresentation contained locally

F08 · The scanner’s “revenue uplift” has the wrong business meaning

The live scanner shows SAR 822.1 as potential revenue uplift for 14 days. Its formula is (own price − cheapest price) × past own units. One row uses (204.70 − 122.43) × 3 = 246.81. At unchanged volume this is revenue lost by cutting the price, not revenue gained. The panel also says matching the lowest price will “beat” that competitor. Missing sales often become zero in the scanner.

Repair: The patch calls this price-gap exposure, explicitly says it is not a forecast, and removes the automatic undercut recommendation. Keep API aliases temporarily but migrate consumers to the explicit new field. A real recommendation needs unit cost, margin limits, comparable available offers and a validated demand model or experiment.

HighLive + source + reproductionRelease blocker

F09 · A successful crawl can still be incomplete

All 11 stores showed successful runs today, but this does not prove catalogue completeness. The shared page-number paginator stops when a page has fewer than 20 items, ignoring declared continuation: a three-page fixture of 12 products returns only 24 of 36. Exceptions break the loop and return partial results. The new soft-block threshold is useful but a partial catalogue above its threshold can still pass. Tier success and product counts alone do not prove complete variants or offers.

Repair: Return a CrawlRun with explicit expected/discovered/fetched/parsed/accepted/rejected counts, continuation exhaustion, errors and completeness. Follow actual pagination metadata, deduplicate stable IDs and detect cursor loops. Do not archive unseen listings or certify a full store refresh after a partial run.

Pinned source: backend/crawlers.py:1199

CriticalSource + reproductionRelease blocker

F10 · Currency, tax, missing prices and aliases are not safely normalized

Competitor normalization ignores effective_price: a fixture with list 100 and effective 75 remains 100, while the own-store shelf helper returns 75. A USD amount is accepted without currency context; missing price becomes 0; NaN is accepted. Arabic decimal ١٢٣٫٤٥ is parsed as 123.0. Canonical suffix stripping lets a carton barcode alias overwrite a unit’s price (20 becomes 240). Unknown own-store tax status is automatically grossed up: merchant 100 becomes 115 with basis merchant_assumed_inc_vat.

Repair: Use Decimal money and explicit currency/tax/price-kind metadata. Reject nonfinite, missing or ambiguous amounts from comparable offers. Source-specific adapters must distinguish effective/list/member/coupon/from-prices. Unknown tax basis stays unknown pending storefront or documented feed verification; do not assume a 15% discrepancy explains every wrong price.

Pinned source: backend/crawlers.py:682; backend/crawlers.py:2830; backend/crawlers.py:3051

HighLive + source + reproductionRelease blocker

F11 · Daily closes and mutable rollups undermine history and window accuracy

Snapshots [10, 5, 10] within one day yield five depletion units in one path but none after daily-close reduction. The ledger writer allows an older event to overwrite a newer same-day close, and replaying the same event increments observations_that_day again. Market Share reads sku_sales_daily, a rebuilt rollup, rather than immutable sealed sales facts. A 30D KSA boundary is formatted as a UTC date for ledger queries/display: the live window was Sep 6–Oct 6 (31 dates), although 30 complete KSA days should be Sep 7–Oct 6. _store_signals also counts no_data store-day rows as days observed.

Repair: Persist immutable observation events with unique event IDs, observation time and ingestion time. Guard late/replayed events; derive KSA days explicitly. Seal versioned daily outputs only after coverage checks. Rebuild corrected derived tables in parallel from retained evidence, then reconcile before switching readers; do not rewrite source history to fit today’s interpretation.

What has actually been built in this investigation

Engineering handoff ZIP contains a unified patch against the Oct 7 commit, original-file SHA-256 manifest, 30-test offline regression suite, reproduction scripts/results, validation logs and application instructions. Structured findings are also supplied.

  • Demo history defaults off and requires an explicit development/test opt-in; demo snapshots carry provenance markers.
  • The shared estimator and daily rollup stop converting missing observations into sales in the reproduced paths. Invalid intervals and large unexplained jumps are withheld rather than clipped into invented measurements.
  • Market Share no longer infers product zero from store capability. It separates “no competitor found” from “competitor sales unknown,” uses a common headline cohort and labels a share using estimated competitors as estimated.
  • The legacy My Products share is withheld with a reason until its denominator is reconciled. Its KPI displays Unavailable, not 0%.
  • Unqualified numeric ranking revenue no longer automatically becomes “exact.”
  • Scanner copy and added API fields distinguish price-gap exposure from a revenue forecast. Stock-depletion labels stop claiming a sales floor.
Validation: 30 targeted offline tests passed. Six changed JSX files passed Babel parsing. Changed Python files passed compilation checks. Tests execute pure modules or exact AST-extracted source functions against in-memory fixtures; they never start the application or touch production.

Limits: this is a containment patch, not a finished rebuild. It does not repair stored history, unmix legacy variants, complete the RBAC migration, certify the current crawler catalogue, or correct every old analytics route. Full FastAPI/Mongo integration, a production frontend build, staging UI checks and order reconciliation remain required. Existing tests that encode the old optimistic behavior will need new expectations.

Deploying it will lower or withhold some metrics. That is intentional. Quarantined/unknown values must remain visible as data-quality gaps rather than being filled with plausible numbers. Existing cached datasets must be versioned or rebuilt in staging, or they can continue serving the old numbers after a code deployment.

The reliable product to build

The main UI can be retained, but its data foundation needs explicit separation between observed prices, actual own transactions, and estimated competitor activity. “Live” must have a stated freshness target and observation timestamp.

1. A canonical catalogue with merchant-scoped offers

Canonical product records describe the trade item: validated GTIN, brand, species/use, formulation/flavour, net amount and unit, pack count, and language aliases. A store listing has its own platform product ID and URL. A variant has its own platform variant ID, attributes and identifiers. SKU is unique only inside its merchant scope unless verified otherwise.

Every match records method, evidence, reviewer, decision time, version and rejection reason. Auto-confirm only when independently collected identity attributes agree. Price similarity is an anomaly signal, not proof of identity. A cheap genuine promotion must not be silently removed simply because several stores charge more.

2. A price observation contract

store_id, listing_id, variant_id, source_url
observed_at_utc, received_at_utc, crawl_run_id
amount_decimal, currency, tax_basis
price_kind: regular | sale | member | coupon | from_price
availability: in_stock | out_of_stock | unknown
quantity_observed: number | null
selected_attributes, pack_count, net_amount, unit
raw_artifact_uri, raw_sha256, extractor_version
validation_status, rejection_reasons, is_synthetic

Use the selected variant’s price and stock from the same observation. Accept JSON-LD or platform responses only when their context is clear; some expose a starting/minimum price or an unselected parent offer. Store list/sale prices separately. Preserve genuine zero quantities; missing price and missing quantity are null. A published/active product is not automatically in stock.

3. One comparison policy

All screens and exports must consume the same eligible-offer result for the same product, selected competitor set, snapshot version and as-of time. Eligibility requires a verified trade-item match, SAR price with compatible tax basis, known observation time inside the configured freshness target, appropriate price conditions, and explicit stock policy. Offer-level reasons explain every exclusion. Changing selected competitors changes the denominator and provenance together.

Keep source price and observation timestamps beside each competitor row. Provide “view source” and “report mismatch.” A history chart shows only actual collected observations; leave gaps between unobserved periods. A daily chart may summarize events, but it must disclose aggregation and keep the original events accessible.

4. Collection that can prove completeness

Use a durable queue with one active run per store, per-host throttling, backoff and bounded retries. Honor source access restrictions and supported feeds. Persist each page’s outcome and continuation token. Separately schedule catalogue discovery and variant price refreshes; frequent refresh of the client’s verified matches should not require rediscovering the whole web every time.

Track expected pages, fetched pages, distinct listings, variant coverage, rejection rate, price/stock parse success and per-offer age. A run is partial until its continuation is exhausted and coverage checks pass. A daily successful run does not refresh offers that were missing from that run. Own-store authorized product/order webhooks can improve timeliness, with periodic reconciliation; competitor webhooks require that merchant’s cooperation.

Do not combine own order totals including shipping/fees with competitor product revenue estimates as if their bases match. Order ingestion needs completeness watermarks so a complete zero-order day is distinguished from an outage. An exact zero must remain zero instead of falling back to a stock estimate.

6. Operations, security and evidence

Separate API, scheduler and collector workers. Add per-route authorization and scoped database access. Expose build/extractor versions, job status and data-quality dashboards. Keep secrets outside images and Git history, encrypt retained credentials, audit manual mappings and configuration changes, and test backup restoration. Monitor parser failures and catalogue-size changes—not only HTTP status.

A controlled route back to client trust

Phase 1 · Preserve evidence and contain misleading output

Confirm the deployed build and consolidate the release branch. Take a restorable production backup and read-only export of products, own catalogue, snapshots, matches, crawl runs, ledger, rollups and order-sync coverage. Rotate publicly exposed secrets. Apply the included patch in staging, enforce API authorization, and clearly suspend unreliable sales/share and automatic pricing recommendations. Deliverable: an auditable baseline and rollback plan.

Phase 2 · Rebuild verified offers for all tracked stores

Implement the variant contract and one comparison API. Start with a stratified 100–200-product acceptance catalogue across all ten competitors, prioritizing the client’s important SKUs and known problem categories: multi-size litter, bags vs cartons, wet-food multipacks, promotions, Arabic decimals, out-of-stock offers and missing fields. The Beso example becomes a permanent regression fixture. Review each source/variant pair manually before widening coverage.

Phase 3 · Reconcile actual own sales and preserve history

Connect the authorized Zid order feed and webhooks, then reconcile daily and line-level totals against the merchant export, including partial refunds and cancellations. Introduce immutable events, idempotency and KSA windows. Quarantine seed/unverifiable history with an audit log; reprocess only retained source evidence into versioned derived collections. If past evidence does not exist, mark historical data unavailable and begin a trustworthy history from the first verified observation.

Phase 4 · Shadow production, then release by evidence

Run the new pipeline alongside the old one for at least two complete collection cycles and a representative promotion/stock-change period. Review discrepancies, reconcile selected-competitor results across every screen, test failure/recovery behavior and restore a backup. Release the verified price product first; enable advanced analytics only after their separate evidence gates pass.

Acceptance gates

  • Price truth: every published comparable offer has variant-level evidence, currency/tax basis, source URL and timestamp. Known fixture mismatches and ambiguity are excluded with explicit reasons. No synthetic observation enters production analytics.
  • Identity: no unreviewed contradictory GTIN/size/pack match in the acceptance catalogue. A new true price drop must survive validation; price consensus alone cannot reject it.
  • Consistency: identical product, competitor filter, as-of time and policy version produce identical eligible sellers, min/max and change values in lists, detail, scanner, exports and alerts.
  • Freshness: choose and measure a service objective—e.g. a proposed 1–4-hour target for priority matched offers and a daily long-tail target where source access permits. Display actual freshness and unknown/blocked states when missed; do not promise instantaneous updates from polling.
  • Orders: complete windows reconcile with merchant exports to the agreed rounding and revenue definition; refunds, retries, out-of-order events and genuine zero-sales days pass.
  • History: replay is idempotent; late events cannot silently change sealed facts; every metric is traceable to a versioned formula and evidence set. UTC/KSA midnight tests cover exactly 7/14/30 requested KSA days.
  • Safety: unauthorized route/object access returns 403; jobs report real completion; failed/partial crawls cannot overwrite verified current offers or archive absent products; backup restore and rollback are demonstrated.
The promise to the client should be: every displayed fact is supported by retrievable evidence; every estimate is labelled; every unknown remains unknown. No honest system can promise zero outages, perfect third-party data or full Saudi transaction coverage from public crawling alone.

What is needed for the next implementation stage

The exact Emergent production build SHA and deployment workflow; a read-only data export or staging copy; approved Zid order integration; and the client’s priority SKU list plus minimum margin rules. Those determine safe migration and reconciliation. Credentials should be configured through the hosting/merchant integration interfaces, not copied into reports or repository files.
